ingredients:
- "1 package refrigerated cheese tortellini approximately 9-12 ounces"
- "1 and 1/2 cups whole milk"
- "3/4 cup heavy cream"
- "2 tablespoons all-purpose flour"
- "1 can (14 ounces) fire-roasted diced tomatoes with juice"
- "3 cloves garlic finely minced"
- "4 cups fresh baby kale roughly chopped, packed"
- "1 teaspoon salt"
- "1/2 teaspoon freshly cracked black pepper"
- "3/4 teaspoon onion powder"
- "1/2 teaspoon garlic salt"
- "1 teaspoon Italian seasoning"
- "1 cup finely grated Parmesan cheese"
instructions:
- "Set a large pot of salted water to boil. Cook tortellini per package instructions, usually 3-5 minutes until floating and tender but not mushy. Drain, toss with a drizzle of oil if not adding right away. Set aside."
- "In a medium bowl, vigorously whisk whole milk, heavy cream, and flour until smooth and no lumps remain. This slurry prevents clumps and helps thicken the sauce."
- "Heat a large wide skillet on medium heat. Add fire-roasted diced tomatoes with juices, stir in minced garlic, chopped kale, salt, pepper, onion powder, garlic salt, and Italian seasoning."
- "Watch closely: when the mixture just starts bubbling and smells bright and garlicky, remove from heat temporarily. Pour in the milk, cream, and flour slurry all at once. Stir aggressively to combine and prevent lumps."
- "Place skillet back over medium-low heat. Sprinkle Parmesan cheese in small batches while continuously stirring. Sauce should thicken and become glossy. Avoid rapid boiling to stop dairy from breaking or separating."
- "Reduce heat to low and let sauce gently bubble and thicken for 3-4 minutes, stirring occasionally. Surface will shimmer and coat the back of a spoon thickly."
- "Add cooked tortellini to sauce. Use tongs or a spatula to fold and fully coat every piece. Taste now and adjust seasoning—salt or pepper as needed. If too thick, splash milk to loosen."
- "Serve hot. Garnish with extra Parmesan or red pepper flakes if desired. Sauce clings to pasta forming creamy pockets of flavor."
introduction: "Tortellini simmered in a creamy tomato sauce with fresh kale instead of spinach. Why kale over spinach? Holds up longer under heat, less likely to get mushy or watery. Fire-roasted diced tomatoes bring a smoky, slightly charred note that shifts this beyond basic marinara. Slurry of milk, cream, and flour thickens the sauce gently, prevents graininess. Parmesan melts in, gives salty umami bite and stabilizes sauce texture. Technique matters here: watch for bubbling, stir at right moment or dairy splits. Toss everything at the end, folding pasta slow enough to keep shape. No dumping or breaking. Timing is flexible but use your eyes and nose, that garlic aroma starts this show."
ingredientsNote: "Whole milk and heavy cream blend smooth texture with richness but lower-fat substitutes like 2% milk or half-and-half work if pared with a touch more flour to thicken. Fire-roasted tomatoes swap regular for smoky depth but use regular diced plus a pinch of smoked paprika for a quick fix. Kale replaces spinach because it resists wilting under heat; if you use spinach, add it late to keep its color and texture intact. Garlic salt and Italian seasoning are reliable flavor lifts, but substitute with fresh basil and parsley if you prefer fresher bright notes. Parmesan must be finely grated — pre-grated lacks moisture, prevents even melting."
